Antwerp

Findings from the CityMove project: A longitudinal spatial data science framework for urban form change in Antwerp

Oberon De Deurwaerder, Lukar Thornton, Edwin Wouters (University of Antwerp) · Job van Exel (Erasmus University Rotterdam)
Poster, Urban Transitions 2026

The environment for walking, playing and cycling is not one thing: it is seven parts, each with its own map, pace of change and events. We rebuilt Antwerp's routine city data for every home to show it.

Three findings

  1. Well provided city-wide, short within a 5 to 10 minute walkMeasured from 121,803 homes along the street network. Beyond dense versus spacious, shortfalls do not come in bundles. Counted the city's way, 95% of residents have a public sports hall within reach; counted from the home, on foot, 41%.
  2. Since 2013 the parts moved apart; the average stayed flatPlaygrounds and sports fields came within reach of more residents, while butchers, bakeries and pharmacies moved out of reach. Averaging the city's nine reach figures discards 88% of that change.
  3. May 2026: one part was cut, and free timetables found itNot told where or when, a scan of monthly public-transport timetables picked out the premetro closure at the right month. "A stop within 500 m", the usual marker, saw nothing.

For monitoring

  • Measure from the home, not the container.
  • Keep the parts apart: averages hide.
  • Detect change on amounts, not on yes/no markers.

About CITY-MOVE

CITY-MOVE studies how cities can support physical activity, with six partner cities: Antwerp, Bogotá, Kampala, Lima, Ljubljana and Rotterdam. Funded by the European Union under Horizon Europe (Grant No. 101136358).
